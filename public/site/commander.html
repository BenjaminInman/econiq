Overview

Recon taught you the cycle. Special Ops taught you to read it. Commander is where you act on it.

Knowing the phase and reading the signals is not the same as making the decision. Most investors still buy with the partners, the asset class, and the debt they used last cycle, operate every year the same way, and end up with a pile of deals rather than a portfolio.

Commander gives every one of those decisions a cycle-first process. The Rule of 6 — who, what, when, where, why, how — applied first to acquisitions and then to the sixty months you spend operating the asset. 2XX2 for rotating capital through one cycle. And the Portfolio Architect for designing what you hold, across every phase, on a single page.

What you'll be able to do

Choose partners, equity, and lenders for the phase you are in, not the last one
Match asset class and strategy to the cycle before you underwrite
Time entry, business plan, and exit to the phase rather than the calendar
Rotate capital through a cycle with 2XX2 and know when a move qualifies
Run rent, renewal, capex, and staffing by phase across the whole hold
Read your own holdings on the five axes of balance and see the concentration
Allocate by phase and rebalance in the right order at the right speed
Produce a one-page portfolio blueprint you can defend to partners

FAQ

Questions.

Do I need Recon and Special Ops first?

Yes. Commander assumes you can name the phase and read the signals that say it is changing. Every lesson applies the four-phase framework and the Onion Framework rather than re-teaching them. Every dollar of Special Ops applies in full toward Commander.

Do I have to take the three parts in order?

No. Full Cycle Acquisitions, 30x Operations, and the Portfolio Architect are all available from day one, and most people start with the part their next decision needs. CMCS certification requires all three and the Workbook.

Is 2XX2 a promise of a 4x return?

No. 2XX2 is a discipline for underwriting capital rotation — two moves inside one cycle, each underwritten to at least 2x on equity. The 2x is a benchmark for whether a move qualifies, not a projection. The lesson is explicit about what the illustrations leave out.

I already own a portfolio. Is Part Three for me?

Especially. The Portfolio Architect starts by reading what you already hold on five axes of balance, then works out what you can change, how fast, and in what order. A rebalancing protocol for an existing portfolio is the core of it.

How long does it take?

Roughly four and a half hours of reading plus the Knowledge Checks, and six to ten hours on the Workbook. Most people run it over six to eight weeks alongside live decisions, which is the point. It is self-paced and access does not expire.
